# Chaos run evidence: the 10-hour night of 2026-09-18

This is the report `tools/chaos.py` wrote at the end of a ten-hour unattended run on the bench
grid, kept verbatim below so the numbers quoted in
[docs/chaos-engineering.md](../chaos-engineering.md) can be checked against their source. Nothing
in it was edited; only this header was added.

## What was under test

Three access points (`node-main`, `node-north`, `node-south`) and three handhelds
(`birthdaysuite`, `hosyond`, `fnk0104b`), each on its own USB serial port. Faults came from the
reset line: a 0.2 s pulse (a crash and immediate restart) or the line held low for 5 s to 10 min
(the device is gone for that long). Handhelds sent each other direct, group and broadcast messages
throughout. LoRa did not exist yet on this run.

**Seed 1172352511.** Re-running with `--seed 1172352511` replays the same faults, on the same slots,
in the same order.

## How to read it

- **55 experiments, 55 recovered.** Every fault healed; none needed a human.
- **Relink median 3.7 s** is the figure that describes a single access point coming back. The
  maxima (30.8 s) come from experiments 12 and 31, where *all three* access points were out at
  once and the whole backbone had to re-form from nothing.
- **Steady state trails relinking** by up to 30 s by design: it waits for each restarted access
  point to print its own 30 s status line before believing it.
- **Handheld failover median 7.3 s** likewise. The p95 and max near 300 s are experiment 31, where
  all three access points were held for 283 s and a handheld had nowhere to fail over to. That is
  the fault being honest, not a defect.
- **46 findings, and they are the point of the run.** 44 are direct messages reported lost between
  handhelds that were registered the whole time, in clusters after an access point restart. They
  were not lost in transit: a restarted access point had never been told who was online and refused
  them. That is the presence bug described in the article, fixed after this run — presence is now
  re-announced every 60 s, and an authenticated frame from a known peer confirms the link from this
  side too. The other 2 findings are the harness noticing that a reset took two attempts to take
  effect on one port.
- **No crash, panic, watchdog or brownout appears anywhere.** The framework records those as
  findings, so their absence means every restart in ten hours was one it caused deliberately.
- **Lowest free heap** is reported in bytes for access points and kilobytes for handhelds, as each
  device's own status line gives it. A value that keeps falling across long uptimes would point at
  a leak; these did not.

Raw hourly serial logs and the full event stream (`raw-NN.log`, `events.jsonl`) stay on the bench:
they run to megabytes and contain grid names and device chatter, so only this summary is published.
