# 2026-10-04, the day: what crashed Fuji, the memory an access point got back, and five clean hours

Everything here was measured on the nine bench boards, all on USB, with the firmware gate on.

## 1. What took an access point's heap to zero

Every `send()` to a handheld takes an MSS-sized buffer (1,512 bytes) and holds it until the handheld
acknowledges. The minute's grid-state announcement and the burst when a handheld registers send several
frames to every attached handheld in one pass, so the cost is handhelds x frames x 1.5 KB at once.

Caught on Everest at 10:05 with the new `[MEM]` line: four attached, 17 KB free, and in the second the
announcement went out the heap touched **1 KB** and the largest free block 3 KB. Denali did the same in a
registration burst. With six attached the same burst needs more than the access point has, which is where
Fuji was when it panicked on 2026-10-03.

## 2. What was changed (D92, D93)

| Change | What it gives |
|---|---|
| The beacon takes the cost of arriving off the heap before it counts room, and is rewritten when room goes to or from zero | no invitation the door refuses; no stale "0 free" |
| A handheld refused by its only access point waits 7, 15, 30, 60 s | no hammering |
| Eight session slots, not fifteen | 7.4 KB |
| NimBLE pools halved, one controller connection | 11 KB |
| No send to a handheld starts below 12 KB free; it waits for the ACKs | the heap cannot be sent to zero |
| The backbone transmit queue is a ring of bytes | about sixty frames in the memory of sixteen |
| ESP-NOW counts as working only when the peer has been heard, not merely acknowledged | LoRa takes over in 6 s, not 20 |
| A LoRa link raised by a data frame stays up | no up/down in the same millisecond |

## 4. Five hours of chaos: `chaos-runs/20261004-121046`, seed 25, 12:10 to 17:11

On the build one step before the final one (send floor at 8 KB, LoRa takeover still at 20 s).

- **21 rounds ran, every one recovered.** No crash, no brownout, no restart that was not asked for.
- **402 of 408** messages delivered with both ends registered; median 0.3 s, worst 2.8 s.
- **36 handheld moves** off a lost access point: median 8.0 s, worst 21.9 s. Nobody was stranded.
- **Two "no heap" refusals** in five hours.
- **The round that crashed Fuji**, on the weakest access point: Fuji and Denali held off for 396 s, all
  six on Everest. Four moved over in 7.7 to 21.9 s; Everest rested at 23 KB, held six sends back for
  212 ms in all and dropped none.
- Alerts round at 15:16 (announcement, SOS, all clear): no finding.

**All six losses were LoRa-only crossings**: five in rounds with ESP-NOW switched off on two access
points, one in the two seconds between ESP-NOW coming back and the link being re-made. With ESP-NOW
working, nothing was lost.

Why LoRa loses, from the run's own counters: in an ESP-NOW-off round each access point's four-slot LoRa
queue fills and drops 2 to 8 frames, and each sends about 25 parts and receives about 10 - three access
points each on the air about 18 s in every 94 are talking over one another. One of the six is the 20 s
ACK-only window, fixed in the final build. The rest need the LoRa leg to carry less or repeat what
matters, which is not built.

## 5. Voice, counted by the handhelds themselves (17:42, final build)

A bench script types `talk` or `demo` at the talker's console; each talker logs frames sent, each listener frames heard and missing. A frame is
100 ms.

| Talker | To | 5 s talk | 10 s talk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Channi, microphone | one handheld | 50 of 50 | 100 of 100 |
| Channi, microphone | group (5 listeners) | 50 of 50 each | 100 of 100 each |
| Channi, microphone | everyone (5 listeners) | 50 of 50 each | 100 of 100 each |
| Jolly, microphone | one handheld | 50 of 50 | 100 of 100 |
| Jolly, microphone | group (5 listeners) | 50 of 50 each | 100 of 100 each |
| Jolly, microphone | everyone (5 listeners) | 50 of 50 each | 100 of 100 each |
| Bluey, generated speech | everyone (5 listeners) | 51 of 51 each | 101 of 101 each |

54 listener results, every frame heard, none dropped before the access point. This is the first test of
group and broadcast voice on the hardware. It says the frames arrived; what it sounded like needs ears.

## 6. Still open

- **LoRa as the only path loses messages** (above). The next piece of work.
- **The link-up catch-up arrives as one burst.** The memory floor now absorbs it (17 sends held back
  for 2.9 s and 3 dropped on Fuji in one round); pacing it would remove the cause.
- **Heap stepping down 5 to 8 KB for tens of minutes** with nobody joining, seen at night. Not seen
  since the memory changes, and not explained.
- **`LG_NODE_GPS_POLLED`** (4 KB on Everest) is off; its bad-sentence count has not been compared with
  the normal reader's.
- **The screen saver's time, battery and message lines** are on the boards and have not been looked at.
- D72 voice notes over LoRa are not built.
