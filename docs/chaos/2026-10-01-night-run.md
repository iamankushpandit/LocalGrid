# Chaos run evidence: the ten hours to flat batteries, 2026-10-01

The run `tools/chaos.py` wrote between **2026-10-01 20:12 and 2026-10-02 06:33**, ended by the access
points running out of battery rather than by a clock. The generated report lives in a gitignored
`chaos-runs/` directory, so the parts worth keeping are reproduced below; the analysis at the top is
added, everything under "The report" is verbatim.

**Seed 54666.** Re-running with `--seed 54666` replays the same faults, on the same slots, in the same
order.

## What was under test

Six handhelds on USB (`fnk0104b`/Channi, `fnk0104b_c`/Jolly, `hosyond`/Bluey, `e28`/Chase, `e28b`/Marshall,
`e40`/Big Pepa) and **three access points on battery**, so no access point could be reached by cable and
none was faulted from this laptop. 45 experiments ran:
**alerts** 5, **hold** 7, **lora_handheld** 18, **no_net** 4, **pulse** 11.

This was the first run with the rounds added that day:

- **`no_net`** switches LoRa off on every handheld that has a module, so Wi-Fi has to stand on its own -
  which is what a handheld shipping without a module will have to do.
- **`alerts`** walks announcement, SOS and the all clear in sequence. The all clear had never been sent on
  this bench before, because the console had no way to send it until that morning.
- **The backup audit**, which runs always and judges every stretch a handheld spends with no Wi-Fi
  session: was its radio switched off on purpose, was it under a fault, was any access point up with
  room? If none of those explains it, the fallback is **unjustified**, and that is a finding whether or
  not a message was lost.

## How to read it: one bug accounts for almost all of it

**182 of the 202 findings are the same bug.** Split the run at the moment the first
outbox filled and the grid is two different machines:

| Period | Clean messages | Delivered |
|---|---|---|
| before the outboxes filled (t < 15569 s) | 403 | **401 (99.5%)** |
| after | 546 | 363 (66.5%) |

Three handhelds went mute and stayed mute: **fnk0104b** 98, **fnk0104b_c** 71, **hosyond** 13
refusals, 176 of them "outbox full"
(6 were the time rule, D6, and
correct). `hosyond` has no LoRa module at all, so this was never a LoRa problem.

**Cause:** a 1:1 message a node *accepts* leaves `LG_OUT_PENDING` for `LG_OUT_ACCEPTED`, and `pick_slot`
held such a slot until the recipient's device confirmed delivery. A recipient whose Wi-Fi is off for a
LoRa round can never confirm, so every message to it held a slot for ever. It is the muteness bug of the
previous day reached by a second road: that fix ages out `PENDING`, and never looks at `ACCEPTED`.

**It also explains eight rounds reporting "not one of N messages it sent while on LoRa alone was
delivered".** That is not a LoRa regression - a handheld that cannot put a message in its outbox cannot
send it over any radio - and it would have been easy to report as one.

**Fixed**, in two tiers: an accepted 1:1 message holds its slot for `LG_ACCEPTED_HOLD_S` (120 s) and
afterwards may be reused *only when the outbox is full*, and an urgent broadcast or an all clear takes the
oldest such slot immediately, because raising an alarm outranks a delivery receipt. Verified on a board:
**2,537 checks, 0 failures**, covered by `test_unconfirmed_direct_does_not_gag_the_handheld`.

### The alerts, and why this mattered

9 of each alert went out. Each was refused three times, and every refusal was this
bug:

| Alert | Sent | Delivered | Refused |
|---|---|---|---|
| announce | 9 | 6 | 3 |
| urgent | 9 | 6 | 3 |
| safe | 9 | 6 | 3 |

**A refused SOS, and a refused all clear, are the worst outcomes this system has.** An alarm nobody can
raise is bad; an alarm nobody can stand down is worse.

Read with care:

- **The three messages lost with both ends registered** are 3 out of 949 clean messages. Too few
  to show a pattern; worth watching rather than chasing.
- **The `no_net` losses cannot be read cleanly this time.** All of those rounds ran after the outboxes had

  the likelier cause than a repeat of the `pick_node` veto, so it is **recorded, not claimed**.
- **The eight "LoRa carried nothing" rounds** are the outbox bug, as above.

## How long a 5000 mAh pack holds an access point

Second measurement, and the first one to be compared against another:

| AP | This run | 2026-09-30 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Everest** | **9 h 19 min** | 9 h 38 min | the only one with a GPS |
| **Fuji** | **12 h 34 min** | 11 h 34 min | |
| **Denali** | **10 h 59 min** | 12 h 42 min | |

**Everest is the shortest both times, by two to three hours.** It is the one difference that stayed put
while Fuji and Denali swapped places between the two runs, which makes "**the GPS costs about two hours**"
the best-supported reading. Fuji and Denali differing by 1.5 h one night and 1.7 h the other, in opposite
directions, is pack variation rather than the board.

Flat is read from silence, not measured: these boards cannot report their own charge, so each figure is
how long that access point kept beaconing. It is a floor on the battery's life, not the battery's life.
