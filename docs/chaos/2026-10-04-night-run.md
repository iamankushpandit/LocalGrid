# 2026-10-04, 05:54 to 09:34: the first run with honest beacons, and the hole it showed

Run `chaos-runs/20261004-055418`, seed 23, planned 4 h, ended at 3 h 39 min when two access-point cables
were moved. Everest and Denali on USB on the D92 build; Fuji watched over BLE because its USB serial had
gone silent, and still running the one-Bluetooth-connection trial image (the run records that as its
first finding).

## What held

- **277 of 277** messages delivered where both ends stayed registered; median 0.3 s, worst 1.9 s.
- **13 rounds ran, every one recovered.** Backbone whole again in a median of 2.0 s, worst 3.2 s.
- **19 of 19** AP-to-AP echoes reached every other access point.
- **No crash, no brownout, no restart that was not asked for**, in 3 h 39 min.
- **One "no heap" refusal in the whole run.** The run before it, without D92, had 18 in its first twenty
  minutes and then stalled for two hours on a stale "0 free".

## The hole: with one access point down, two handhelds had nowhere to go

| Round | Denali held for | Its two handhelds were without Wi-Fi for | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| #4 | 585 s | 583 s, 583 s | every AP they could hear was full |
| #8 | 380 s | 382 s, 382 s | the same |
| #10 | 404 s | 412 s, 422 s | the same |
| #2 | 16 s | 7 s (both moved to Fuji) | Fuji had room at that moment |

Everest and Fuji each said "2 attached, 0 free", and it was true: Everest rests at 16 KB with two, and
the room sum needs 17.9 KB before it offers a slot. So the grid had six slots for six handhelds. D92 made
the beacons honest; it did not make the access points bigger. The 14 messages lost in the run all had an
end on one of those stranded handhelds. Neither has a LoRa module, so they had no second path.

This is the result that matters for resilience: **losing one access point of three must not strand
anybody**, and on this memory it does.

## What was done about it the same morning

- **Eight session slots instead of fifteen** (`NODE_SESS_MAX`): 7.4 KB of RAM that was reserved for
  sessions no AP could serve. Measured on Fuji alone with all six handhelds: 34 KB free at rest where
  there were 27, **six handhelds served** where there were four, 18 KB free with all six, low-water 5 KB,
  one refusal in five minutes.
- Still waiting, because the session's permission check refused the edit to the build configuration: the
  two Bluetooth settings measured the night before (11 KB more), and turning the GPS switch on (4 KB on
  Everest).

## Other findings

- **"LoRa peers did not come back within 150 s" (twice) was the harness.** The firmware keeps its peer
  table across a module reset and logs no "Link up"; the harness cleared its own copy and waited for one.
  Fixed.
- **The backbone's transmit queue overflows.** Everest: `tx 294 tx_fail 8 dropped 78` ten minutes after a
  boot. Those are frames dropped because all 16 slots were full, most likely in the catch-up flood when
  links come up. Not chased yet.
- **Everest's low-water mark reached 3 KB** with two handhelds attached, without a crash.
- A harness crash when a cable is pulled mid-run (a reset pulse on a port that had gone) is fixed.
